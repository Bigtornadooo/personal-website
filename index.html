01

Live-Sync

Huawei Tech4City Hackathon, Jul 2026

An AI lecture-comprehension platform that lets a student ask questions of a lecture as it happens. The genuinely interesting problem was cost: routing questions dynamically between models cut inference spend by over 80% with no measurable drop in quality. Pitched to judges on the accessibility track.

Speech-to-text Vision-language model Vector database Model routing
Stage 1

Sign up

Two steps and a student or professor account is ready. No setup call, no onboarding deck.

Stage 2

Upload, then go live

A professor uploads the slide deck once, then presses Go Live. Live-Sync pre-indexes the deck before the lecture even starts.

Stage 3

Listen and transcribe

The professor just talks. Live-Sync is already listening, transcribing the lecture live through VideoDB, no typed prompts, no raised hand.

Stage 4

A student joins

A student enters the code on screen and gets the live transcript, with one button to catch up if they lost the thread.

Stage 5

Ask anything

Answers come straight from that lecture's own slides and transcript, not a generic model response. Routing every question between a lightweight model and a flagship one is what keeps this affordable at scale.

0%+ cut from inference cost, the real result of routing this way
Stage 6

The room signals back

Students flag lost, too fast, or right pace in real time, so the professor can see where the room actually is without stopping to ask.

Stage 7

One home for every module

Every module lands in one dashboard: jump into whatever lecture is live, or replay any chapter that already happened.

Claim!

IBM Z Datathon, Oct 2026

An explainable insurance-claim risk scorer for claims officers, built by a team of six in one day. He built the product layer: a FastAPI service and web UI that score a claim with the team’s tuned XGBoost model, return a fast-track, review or investigate tier, and show each field’s TreeSHAP contribution. Containerised on an IBM LinuxONE (s390x) server, with an offline PDF claim reader that fills all 19 fields and re-scores after every sentence. A blank policy type changed the tier for 36.7% of claims, so he made it required and added score ranges and a what-if toggle.

What I run on

Sport, mostly

I have loved sport my whole life, and the two athletes I keep coming back to were never the most gifted men in the room. They were the ones who refused to be out-worked. That is the only competitive advantage I have ever seen hold up.

Cristiano Ronaldo, Forward

The repetition is the point.

He arrived at Sporting as a skinny winger who was not even the best player in his own academy, and turned himself into the most relentlessly conditioned athlete in the sport by treating every session as non-negotiable: the extra hour after training, the diet, the sleep, for two decades without a gap. He is still there and still scoring in his forties, because he never once let the standard slip and called it a rest day.

Talent without working hard is nothing.

Cristiano Ronaldo

Kobe Bryant, Guard

Fall in love with the process.

The Mamba Mentality was never a slogan, it was a schedule: in the gym before dawn, counting makes rather than shots, studying footwork frame by frame, chasing the small edge that compounds while everyone else is still asleep. He measured himself against the work instead of the scoreboard, which is why the losses never moved him and the championships never quite satisfied him either.

Mamba mentality is all about focusing on the process and trusting in the hard work when it matters most.

Kobe Bryant

Linklater shot it over twelve years with the same cast, and let the ordinary days do the work.

Ellar Coltrane was six years old when filming started and eighteen when it finished. There is no villain in it, no twist, and no scene where somebody’s life visibly turns. It is just a kid’s normal weeks (car journeys, house moves, arguments, a first job) stacked one on top of another until you look up and realise an entire person has been assembled out of them.

That is why it is my favourite, and it is the same argument Ronaldo and Kobe make, only told quietly. Nobody in it gets a breakthrough moment. You never feel the day that changes you; you only ever see it afterwards, in the aggregate. Which is exactly why I care more about what I do on an ordinary Tuesday than about what I would do if I were inspired. And probably why the app I actually use every day is a planner about ordinary days.

Twelve years, one cast No plot, on purpose Compounding
